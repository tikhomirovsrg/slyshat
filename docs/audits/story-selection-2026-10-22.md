# Selection and expansion of the story catalogue

Status: research and authoring in progress. Research plans are not counted as
published lessons or ready recordings.

## Requested result

150 active distinct stories in English and 150 in Italian, with 30 per level
A1–C1. The curation audit retained 27 existing stories per language; this release
therefore needs 123 new stories per language. Old IDs, saved routes, answers and
recordings remain accessible without appearing as new catalogue entries.

## Sources and adaptation

Five independent research partitions own different primary-source collections.
Candidates record a source work, author, verified URL, source-plot outline and
new premise, decisions and consequences. The source work is used once across
the expansion. Human characters, original dialogue and new branches adapt a
plot mechanism; modern copyrighted translations are not copied. Individual
source provenance is retained in each authored story.

The selection draws on early editions/primary texts in Project Gutenberg and
Wikisource, including Aesop (V. S. Vernon Jones, 1912), O. Henry, Saki and Italian
novellas. Website metadata, editorial material and translations have their own
rights; a public-domain underlying work does not make every page unrestricted.

## Duplicate review

Two passes are required: after selection and after the complete dialogue and
branches have been written. The comparison considers the problem, causal
sequence, choice and payoff, not the title, names, topic or CEFR label.

Automatic checks reject repeated IDs, semantic-key strings, source-work keys
and identical complete dialogue. A lexical similarity report queues pairs for
manual review against both new plans and the retained catalogue. A low score
does not prove uniqueness; each partition also receives an editorial review.

Early rejections:

- A purported ancient prototype authenticated by a date/signature repeated the
  retained museum/photo investigation mechanism; replacement requested.
- A chance encounter identifying an old family chair from a photo repeated the
  same mechanism; replacement requested.
- Its first replacement, an actor stuck in an oversized teapot costume,
  collided with the A1 inflated-costume doorway story; replacement requested.
- Two additional visitors mistaken for an expert repeated the C1 accidental
  critic. They were replaced by rewarded attempts to escape a performance and
  a negotiator receiving half of an unexpectedly practical reward.
- The Italian accidental-fame proposal repeated the English viral kettle
  overshadowing a craftsperson. The replacement uses an awkward invitation
  disguised as a scholarly argument about weather.
- A mentor taking credit for a student's work appeared twice. One was replaced
  by secret practical help that prevents the learner from becoming independent.
- Two objects distracting a speaker were too close. The A1 replacement uses an
  accidentally rung service bell revealing a loose chair leg.

The final 246-plan structural check finds no repeated IDs, source-work keys or
plot-fingerprint strings. This is a research milestone, not proof that the
lessons are ready. Full-dialogue review is still in progress. During that review,
paint accidentally marking a shirt was found too close to painting somebody's
trousers without permission; the latter needs another premise before authoring.

Lexical review also surfaced four non-duplicates: a ball raised in a vase versus
an empty drinking glass repeatedly washed as an indirect request; a coat being
honoured instead of its wearer versus fabric caught in a folding chair; seasonal
coat lining versus two garments accidentally joined by a seam; and a forgotten
partnership agreement versus a ceremony attempting to obtain agreement too soon.
These share objects or vocabulary, but their causes, decisions and payoffs differ.

## Level review

The [Council of Europe CEFR Companion Volume (2020)](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)
guides the editorial review: concrete familiar information and clear delivery
at A1/A2; connected factual narratives and reasons at B1; more complex argument
and contextual interpretation at B2; implied stance and register at C1.
Word count and playback rate are diagnostic measurements, not CEFR certificates.

Each story records grammatical demands and a listening objective. Advanced
questions must require an inference grounded in an actually heard line. Simple
stories must remain intelligible without grasping sophisticated irony. Three
useful expressions from the opening prepare the learner without revealing an
ending. Branch questions only reference the chosen branch.

## Release safeguards

The expansion is separate from frozen course/cast/SQLite releases. New audio is
bound to exact text, actor, voice, pace policy and source checksums. Cache-only
synthesis cannot publish a readiness manifest. Production requires all new
dialogue and answers, valid media bounds and checksums, and complete graphs.
Technical validation does not replace expert listening or learner calibration.
